Denver offices and small businesses

Moving an office in Denver: a planning guide for people who still have to run the business

In short. An office move is a project with a deadline. The movers are a small slice of it: you also have the building, the network, the paperwork with the state and city, and the day your staff have to work. This guide is organized as a schedule, with the official sources for the government pieces.

Weeks are our planning model, not a legal or industry rule. Shrink it for a three-person office; stretch it for anything with a server room.

Government items that people forget

What do I have to update with the Colorado Secretary of State when my office moves?

If your entity files a periodic report, the report is where the principal office address and registered agent information get updated, and you can file up to two months before or two months after your report month without penalty. An entity that does not file becomes delinquent.

Does a Denver business need to update its sales tax accounts after a move?

Denver and Colorado keep separate sales tax accounts, so an address change may need to go to both. Denver's FAQ describes using the eBiz Tax Center, an email to 311, or a letter with your return; Denver does not take account or address changes by phone.

The full list with links is on the address-change checklist.

Why do an office move on paper first

Every dollar of surprise on an office move traces to a missing line in the scope: a freight elevator that was only available until 5, a conference table that had to be disassembled, a vendor who thought "server room" meant two racks, not nine. Write the scope as a table and have every bidder price the same table.
