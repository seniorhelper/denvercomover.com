Office move guide

The office move timeline, with owners

Use this as a template. The weeks and owners are our suggestions; adapt them to your size.

Name four owners

OwnerResponsible for
Move leadSchedule, vendor contracts, daily decisions, the walk-through
IT leadNetwork, servers, phones, backups, power
Facilities contactBoth buildings' rules, elevator, parking, keys and access cards
Staff coordinatorPacking rules, labels, who sits where, communications

In a very small office these can be one person. Still write the four lists separately.

Milestones

  1. Lease and date locked. Confirm when you get keys, and when you must be out of the old space.
  2. Floor plan approved. Mark each workstation and shared item. Movers can place furniture only if the plan exists.
  3. Estimates in. Ask every bidder to price the same written scope.
  4. Insurance and building paperwork submitted.
  5. Packing begins with archives and rarely used items.
  6. Final inventory. What is going, what is being recycled, what is being sold.
  7. Move. Then a punch list the next morning.

Budget lines people forget

  • Overlap rent on the old and new space.
  • Elevator reservation fees or deposits in either building.
  • Internet and phone installation fees and any temporary service.
  • Cabling, mounting TVs and monitors, and reassembly of workstations.
  • Updated signage, business cards, letterhead and website address.
  • Disposal of what you are not taking. Business trash does not go in Denver's residential Large Item Pickup, which is for households; the city page lists commercial and industrial waste as not accepted.

The day after

Schedule a half-day of quiet time. People need to find the stapler. Ask staff to report issues in one place, and hold the movers to any damage claim window listed in your contract.

Sources and notes (read 11 Nov 2026)
  • Denver Solid Waste: Large Item Pickup (lists commercial and industrial waste as not accepted)
  • Roles and milestones are planning suggestions from this site, not requirements.
