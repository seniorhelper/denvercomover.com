Business address changes: state, city, federal

Verify before filing. Agencies change forms and fees. Use the linked pages and keep proof of each filing. This is information, not tax or legal advice.

Colorado Secretary of State

The Secretary of State's periodic report FAQ says the report updates the principal office address and registered agent information, and that filing confirms the existing information was reviewed even if nothing changed. Your due month appears as the "Periodic report month" on your entity's summary page in the business database. You may file up to two months before or two months after that month without a penalty, and an entity that does not file becomes delinquent. File electronically on the Secretary of State's website. The page refers to the state's fee schedule for amounts, so check it directly.

Not covered here. Whether a standalone change filing is better than waiting for your report depends on your entity. Check with the Secretary of State or your attorney.

Denver sales and use tax account

Denver's Business Tax FAQ says you can change the address on your Denver sales/use tax account online through the eBiz Tax Center, by emailing 311@denvergov.org, or by mailing a letter with your return. Include your account number on every request. Phone requests for account and address changes are not accepted.

Denver's FAQ also says that a business located in Denver that makes retail sales or leases or rents tangible personal property, or sells certain services, needs a Denver sales tax license. Read the FAQ to see whether that applies to you.

Colorado Department of Revenue

Colorado's state sales tax account is separate. The Department of Revenue says you can update a mailing address in Revenue Online, or submit its DR 1102 Address or Name Change Form. For a physical location, use the account number and site number from your sales tax license. Make sure you are using the current version of the form from tax.colorado.gov.

IRS

The IRS has Form 8822-B for changing a business's address or responsible party. Check the form's instructions on irs.gov for the filing deadline and where to send it.

USPS and everyone else

  • File a USPS change of address for the business, and check that the forwarding start date matches your move date.
  • Update your bank, insurer, payroll and benefits providers, and professional licenses.
  • Update your website, Google listing, invoices, email signatures and customer notices.
