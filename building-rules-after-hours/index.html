Office move guide

Building rules, certificates of insurance and after-hours moves

Not a legal standard. Office towers and business parks write their own move rules. This is the question list to take to both property managers.

Get both buildings' rules in writing

Ask the property manager at the old and new addresses for the tenant move policy. It normally sets hours, elevator use, protection requirements, insurance and who to notify.

The usual questions

QuestionWhy
What are the permitted moving hours? After-hours or weekend only?Many towers keep freight elevators for tenants after business hours, which drives the schedule and may add labor cost.
Is a certificate of insurance required from the mover and from the tenant? What limits and what wording?The mover requests the certificate from its insurer; send the building's exact wording at booking.
How is the freight elevator reserved, and is there a deposit?Shared elevators are the bottleneck. A gap between slots can idle a crew.
Where does the truck load, and what are the clearances?Dock heights, ramps and garage clearances limit truck size.
Is security or an engineer required on site? Who pays?A building may need staff present for after-hours access.
What floor and wall protection is required?Some buildings require floor covering, wall padding and door protection.
Are there rules on removing old furniture and trash?Dumpsters may be restricted; confirm who removes leftovers.

On-street trucks

When the building has no dock, the truck may have to stand on the street. Denver's transportation department handles Street Occupancy permits via E-Permits, and an online meter bagging system whose requests take up to 72 hours to process. Its Street Occupancy page does not say whether a moving truck needs one, so confirm with the city when planning.

After-hours in practice

  • Add the after-hours premium to your estimate comparison so you compare totals, not hourly rates alone.
  • Give staff a plan for the next morning: where to find things, who handles problems.
  • Leave time for the building to inspect common areas and sign off.

Your old landlord

Your lease may require restoration of the space, patching, removal of cabling or a final inspection. Read the lease before booking the last day of the move.
