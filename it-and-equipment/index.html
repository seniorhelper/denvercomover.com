Office move guide

Moving servers, networks and the equipment that runs the office

Scope note. This is a planning guide from general practice, not a technical standard. Your IT provider or the equipment maker has the final word on handling.

Decide what kind of move it is

TypeWhat it means
CutoverStand up the new site first, copy data over, switch on a set date. Least downtime, most duplicate cost.
ForkliftPower down, truck it, power up. Cheapest, but needs a defined downtime window.
Cloud-firstMove services off local servers before the office move so the physical move is only laptops and phones.

Backups and restore tests

  • Take a full backup, store a copy off the building, and confirm you can restore a sample file before anything is unplugged.
  • Record admin credentials and licenses in a secure place that does not depend on the servers you are moving.

Internet and phones come first

Installation of business internet and phone service can take longer than the move itself. Place the order as soon as the lease is signed, and ask the carrier for a written install date. Have a plan for the first day if it slips, such as phone hotspots.

Labeling and photographing

  • Photograph rack fronts and rears and every cabled port before disconnecting.
  • Label cables on both ends. Number each device and match it to the new rack plan.
  • Keep small parts like rails, screws and power cords in labeled bags attached to the device.

Ask the mover

  • Has the crew handled racks and network gear before, and do they use padding rather than loose-loading?
  • Will the equipment travel in a vehicle with climate control, and for how long?
  • Who is liable for equipment damage, and what valuation applies? Get the answer in writing.

Power and altitude

Check that the new space has the circuits and cooling your equipment needs before moving day. Denver sits about a mile above sea level; some equipment makers publish altitude limits for cooling and drives, so check the manual rather than assuming.

Sources and notes (read 11 Nov 2026)
  • General practice guidance; no government source applies. Check equipment manuals for handling and altitude specifications.
