Choosing a commercial mover for a Denver office

How the companies we refer are screened: a Colorado PUC household-goods permit, insurance and a BBB A+ rating are confirmed by us first. The checklist below is what you should verify on top of that.

Start with a written scope

Give every bidder the same document: addresses, floors, elevator access, headcount, workstation count, special equipment, disassembly, disposal and the time window. Ask them to price against it. Offers that differ wildly usually priced different jobs.

Verify registrations yourself

  • Ask which state and federal registrations the company holds and the numbers. For a household-goods mover in Colorado, the Public Utilities Commission issues permits; permit numbers should be listed on its ads.
  • We have not confirmed which Colorado permit, if any, a company needs for a purely commercial move, so ask the mover to state in writing what it holds and look it up with the issuing agency.
  • For moves across state lines, ask for the USDOT number and check it on the federal motor carrier safety administration's lookup tools.

Insurance, in two parts

  1. Liability coverage you give your building. The mover's certificate of insurance, with the wording the building specifies.
  2. Coverage for your own equipment. Ask what valuation applies if a monitor, server or filing cabinet is damaged, and what the claim process and deadline are. Check your own business property policy too.

Estimate type

EstimateWhat to confirm
Fixed price for the written scopeWhat counts as a change to scope and how it is priced.
Hourly with crew and truckMinimum hours, travel time, overtime or after-hours rates, and a cap if available.
Per-item or per-workstationWhether disassembly and reassembly are included.

Questions for the reference call

  • Have you moved offices of my size in the last year?
  • Who is the crew lead, and will that person be on site?
  • What happens if the freight elevator is late?
  • What is your damage claim process, and who handles it?

Red flags

  • A quote with no walk-through or written scope for a job with specialty equipment.
  • Large deposit demands or cash-only terms.
  • No clear answer on registrations, or a refusal to put them in writing.
